# Deterministic batch tactics policy

Batch mode uses one deterministic tactics policy for both sides of an encounter. It evaluates legal move-plus-action plans together and chooses the highest-scoring plan. The score starts with expected damage, control value and survival value weighted 1.0 each; resource cost is 0.0 because batch measures one encounter. These named constants, ordering rules and thresholds are policy inputs that standing assumptions may override.

Target priority considers the next opponent action cycle: expected damage, reliable control, healing, and recharge or limited-use effects. Immediate expected damage breaks ties, followed by lowest current hit-point percentage and stable combatant ID. Downed combatants are not legal targets unless explicitly enabled. Limited-use abilities are spent whenever they score higher than the best at-will action; recharge abilities are available only after their rules-based recharge succeeds. Legendary actions are spent in the current eligible window when useful and never carried into a later round. Amended by ADR 0016: that window lies between the end of one turn and the start of the next, each holder takes at most one use per window, and the engine's eligibility rule decides who may act.

Movement is selected only as part of a plan that enables or improves an action, using the step engine's established band costs. Area effects use default target counts of 1 for single target, 2 for a small area, 3 for a medium area, and 4 or all eligible combatants for a large or battlefield-wide area. The policy includes ally harm and wasted damage in the score and uses a smaller area when its net value is better. Area counts are logged and overrideable.

The healer revives a downed ally when revival is legal and provides meaningful expected contribution before the encounter ends; otherwise revival competes normally with healing and attacks. Living allies below 50% hit points are healed only when the expected benefit exceeds the best attack or control action and the ally is likely to be downed before its next turn. Non-healers use consumables below 25% hit points, under lethal incoming threat before their next turn, or when needed to restore an incapacitated ally. Modeled variable effects use expected value; unmodeled consumables are unavailable to batch.

Reactions (policy v3, ticket 046). Batch encounters open reaction windows (ADR 0018), and the policy decides each one on the state the engine presents there, one window at a time with no look-ahead. On a hit, it takes a reaction that adds n to AC (Shield, Parry) only when the attack total is below AC + n and the d20 is not a natural 20, so the reaction turns that hit into a miss. Among several that do, it takes one with no usage limit or slot first, then the lowest slot, then stat-block order. On an Opportunity Attack window, it takes the at-will melee attack with the highest expected damage against the mover, capped at the mover's hit points and plus the mover's threat when the attack would drop it, but only when that value is strictly above the reactor's reaction reserve. The reserve is `min(AC_POINT_VALUE × n × D, H)` for the reactor's best ready hit reaction, where D is the expected attack-roll damage its opponents' best ready actions deal it through the reaction's filter and H is the largest average hit among those attacks. `AC_POINT_VALUE` is 0.1, the value of one AC point against one round of incoming attack damage, a code constant and not a `policy.*` input. A mover's step-out penalty is the summed value of the attacks the policy would take against it from reactors that pass the engine's eligibility rule; ranged step-outs and teleports read the same function. The policy never plans the Disengage action. Each decided window logs a reason line, held reactions included.

Buffs, extra actions and forced movement (policy v3, tickets 045, 055, 056 and 075). A timed modifier among an ability's top-level effects is valued over a horizon of at most `BUFF_HORIZON` (2) rounds: its rounds when shorter, the whole horizon for concentration and longer durations, 0 for a duration that ends within the turn. The horizon `h` is cut to the rounds the fight has left: the actor's active opponents' hit points over the summed damage threat (threat without healing, each foe's share capped at its hit points) of the actor's allies that can act, the actor included, or `BUFF_HORIZON` when that damage is 0; `h` may be fractional. A concentration modifier's rounds are weighted by `k`, the chance the actor keeps concentration through one round of opponent turns, and every other modifier uses `k = 1`. Each opponent that can act counts damage on the actor from its ready action with the most of it: attack-roll and save damage when it ranks the actor first, only saves inside areas otherwise. When that damage summed over opponents reaches the actor's hit points, `k = 0`; otherwise `k` is the product of `(1 - p_fail(max(10, floor(a / 2))))^(d / a)` over counted attack damage `d` with average hit `a`, and `1 - p_fail(max(10, floor(s / 2)))` over counted save damage `s`, where `p_fail` is the actor's chance to fail the Constitution save from the same sources the engine rolls with. The rounds counted are `Σ_{i=1..ceil(h)} k^(i - f) × clamp(h - (i - 1), 0, 1)`, with `f = 1` when the bearer's next turn comes before any able opponent's in initiative order after the actor (never for the actor's own modifiers) and `f = 0` otherwise, so a safe caster in a long fight still counts 2. Legendary actions and Opportunity Attacks are left out of `k`, and `d / a` is a fixed hit count used as an exponent. On its bearer, AC +n is worth `A × AC_POINT_VALUE × n` and Advantage on saving throws `S × SAVE_ADVANTAGE_VALUE` (0.3), both as survival, where A is the attack-roll damage the bearer's opponents' best ready actions deal it (the D of the reaction reserve, unfiltered) and S is the damage from their saves of the modifier's ability, each opponent using its best ready action for such saves. An attack-roll bonus +n is worth `threat × AC_POINT_VALUE × n` as damage. An extra action is worth, as damage, the bearer's best expected damage from one action the extra action allows against its top-priority opponent, and 0 while the bearer can't act, as in Haste's lethargy. Speed, Reactions, damage-roll and other modifiers score 0, and so do temporary hit points and roll modifiers. A `self` modifier is valued on the actor. For a `target` modifier the policy values the actor and each living ally, every ally counting as in range, and casts on the best, ties to the actor and then the lower id; the plan reason names the bearer, as `haste on standard-striker-11 (+2 AC against 51.5 attack damage, advantage on dex saves against 0.0 dex save damage, extra action 21.1)`. A bearer that already has a modifier from the same `source_ability` scores 0 for it. A modifier under a save's `on_failure` or an attack's `on_hit` lands on an opponent and scores 0; debuff values are not decided. A concentration spell is not cast while the caster's concentration still holds an opponent's condition or any modifier, so a Haste is never recast onto a second ally. Haste's lethargy is scored: a concentration modifier whose `on_end` gives the bearer a condition that stops it acting (Incapacitated, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned or Unconscious) costs `threat(bearer) × (1 - k^h)` as damage while the bearer can act, so a plan can score below 0. The plan reason adds `<rounds> of <h> rounds at keep <k>` when `k < 1` or `h` is below the modifier's horizon, and `lethargy -<cost>` when there is a cost. In batch, a combatant with an extra action asks the policy for an extra-action plan after its action and bonus action: the best action the engine's extra-action check allows, with movement only from what the action left, and never Disengage. A push of 5 feet or more scores `threat(target) × f` as control, where f is 1.0 for a push of 35 feet or more on a target whose walk speed is under 35 and 0.25 otherwise; a pull, or a target with any attack (actions and bonus actions) that doesn't need engagement, scores 0. These are code constants, not `policy.*` inputs.

Teleports and roll sources (policy v3, ticket 073). A teleport scores, as survival, the step-out penalty of the opponents engaged with the actor where the plan leaves it: the Opportunity Attacks it avoids, counting only those the policy would take. It scores 0 when nothing is engaged or the plan already steps out, and it never unlocks a melee plan. Ticket 045's sum of every engaged foe's best attack is replaced. The penalty and the reaction reserve return 0 at the nesting limit, which stops a teleport that reaches its own penalty through `threat`. An attack's Advantage or Disadvantage is the engine's (`disposition_with`, ADR 0017), with "within 5 feet" read from where the plan leaves the actor (engaged after a move in, not after a step-out) and the policy's own close-combat Disadvantage on ranged attacks added as one more source, all combined by the two-flag rule: any Advantage and any Disadvantage make a straight roll. A save's success chance reads the engine's automatic Strength and Dexterity failures, save disposition and timed save bonuses, so a Paralyzed target fails a Dexterity save for certain.

The policy logs its reason, target, resource use, movement and area assumptions. If a plan becomes illegal, it recomputes from the updated state; if none is legal, it takes the standard defensive or no-op action and logs that outcome. Its stable identifier and version, including policy constants, are part of baseline fingerprints so changing the policy makes prior baseline cells stale.

## Considered options

- Agent-selected batch actions: rejected because batch is one engine call and needs reproducible results without per-turn agent decisions.
- Random or resource-conserving tactics: rejected because they add variance or optimize for a future encounter that does not exist inside one batch fight.
- Separate movement and action heuristics: rejected because the best action can depend on the range band reached and the movement cost.
- Fixed area counts without logging: rejected because abstract engagement cannot recover exact geometry and the assumption must remain visible and overrideable.

## Consequences

- Baseline results are comparable across fixtures because both sides use the same named policy and version.
- Policy constants and scoring weights are part of the standing-assumption surface and must be included in logs and baseline fingerprints.
- The policy is intentionally an encounter optimizer, not a model of intelligent long-term resource management.
- More complex tactics, including multi-monster coordination and future encounter resource planning, require a new policy decision rather than silent changes to this one.
