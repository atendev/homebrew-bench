# Spell lift as the spell value metric

The batch report compares a homebrew spell with the SRD spells of its level. Ticket 001 of the spell-testing map ([Spell value metric](../../.plan/closed/001-spell-value-metric.md)) chose what that comparison is measured in. The verdict is spell lift: how much the party's win rate and casualty rate change when the spell goes onto the caster role. It is not damage per cast.

## Considered options

- **Expected damage or healing per cast, per slot level.** Rejected as the verdict. It is cheap and needs no fights, but it gives no number for control or buff spells such as Hold Person, Bless or Haste, and damage and healing can't share one scale. It stays in the report as an ungraded diagnostic.
- **Expected damage or healing per slot spent.** Rejected. It has the same gaps, and comparison across levels buys little because the report compares same-level spells only.
- **Change in win rate with the spell added to a caster.** Adopted as spell lift. It puts every spell kind on one scale and reuses the monster baseline's engine, standard party and batch tactics policy (ADR 0011).

## Consequences

- **Swap, not add.** The caster role keeps its slot table, but every spell it knows of the tested spell's level is replaced by the tested spell. Every spell goes on the caster role whatever its class list, so all share one attack bonus and save DC. The control run removes that level's spells. The rest of the caster's list stays, so the tested spell competes for concentration as it would in play. The policy chooses when and on whom to cast. A spell the policy never casts in a row reads "never cast", not zero.
- **Spell panel.** Each party level has a fixed, fingerprinted panel of baseline-curve fixtures. A fixture qualifies when the control party wins about 40 to 80% of fights against it, measured in the control run. The panel has a single-monster shape and a group shape (several copies of a lower-CR fixture), so area and multi-target spells show their worth. The single-monster shape includes a spellcasting fixture where the curve has one. Where it has none, the row says so and counter spells read "not measured". As built (spell-testing ticket 012), panels cover the odd party levels 1 to 17, which hold every row below. Each panel is selected with the control for its headline spell level, (P+1)/2. A row at the same party level for another spell level, such as a cantrip row, fights the same fixtures, but its lift is still taken against its own control, the one without that row's level. The 40 to 80% band holds for the headline control only, so that row's control may win more or less often. Each shape keeps up to three fixtures in the band, nearest 60% first. The spellcasting fixture must be in the band too, since every panel member must. The group shape is four copies of a fixture the control party beats more than 80% of the time alone, with a CR below every single-shape fixture's. The panel is committed as `data/baseline/spell-panel.json`, and `bench baseline panel` regenerates it.
- **Rows.** The headline row is party level 2L-1, the first level with a slot of the spell's level L, cast at level L. A spell with an upcast rule also gets one row per cast level N above L, at party level 2N-1. Cantrips get rows at party levels 1, 5, 11 and 17. Each row is placed against the SRD spells of the same level run at the same row. An upcast clause the engine can't run is marked not measured.
- **Placement, not pass/fail.** The report gives the spell's place among same-level SRD spells and names its nearest neighbours. Placement uses the mean lift across the panel, and the single-monster and group columns are shown too. A "reads like level N" line appears only when the spell falls outside its own level's SRD range. A row with fewer than three modeled SRD spells is thin data and gets no percentile.
- **Noise.** Each panel fight runs a fixed number of times with paired seeds (ADR 0007): the control and every spell run share a seed per fight. The report gives an interval on the lift. SRD neighbours whose intervals overlap the homebrew's are "level with" it, not above or below.
- **Fight count and interval, as built (spell-testing ticket 013).** Each panel member fights 100 times in every row, the panel's own count, so a six-member row pools 600 paired fights. The lift is the mean of per-fight differences: win (1 or 0) with the spell minus win without it, and likewise for a fight with at least one party death. The interval is the mean plus and minus 1.96 standard errors of those paired differences, over every panel fight in the row. The report can say it in one sentence: two spells are level with each other when their 95% intervals on the mean win-rate lift overlap. The single-monster and group columns are the same mean and interval over that shape's fights. A fight that reaches the round cap is a stalemate and counts as not won, for the control and the spell alike, as in the panel's own win rates. Lift is signed: a spell that makes the party lose more often, such as one that replaces a better spell of its level, reads below zero.
- **The row's own control, as built.** Each row builds its control with `spell_panel::control_party(party, row_level, book)` and fights each member with the member's stored `seed` and `copies`, so the control fight and the spell fight at one index share their seed. A row reports its control's pooled win rate and whether it sits in the 40 to 80% band. Only the headline control is selected into the band, so an upcast or cantrip row's control may fall outside it, and the row says so instead of hiding it.
- **What goes on the caster, as built.** The tested spell replaces every ability of the caster that casts it, at any level, then goes into the section its `casting_time` names, cast at the row's slot level (`upcast: true` above its own level). A cantrip is at-will with no slot. A reaction spell with no trigger of its own is cast when its caster is hit by an attack roll; a counter spell is cast when an enemy casts a spell. A row reads "not measured" for a `long` spell, for an SRD spell's upcast row when the spell has an upcast clause the engine doesn't apply, and for a counter spell where the panel has no spellcasting fixture. A homebrew upcast clause is played as its standing assumption says. A spell the policy never casts in any fight of the row reads "never cast".
- **Per-cast diagnostic, as built.** Batch counts the party's casts per spell, and credits each with the damage and healing the caster's own events show before the next cast. Damage or healing that lands in later rounds, such as a wall or a summoned weapon, is not credited, so the figure is a diagnostic and not the verdict.
- **Party side only.** Batch measures the spell as a party tool. A monster that casts it is measured by monster testing, and live mode covers both sides in play. The report says this.
- **Nothing to measure.** An entry that can't be modeled is refused at intake and never reaches batch. Intake also refuses a homebrew spell with no combat effect (ADR 0021). "Not measured", with the reason and never zero, is for an SRD spell with no combat effect the engine runs, and for an accepted spell batch can't cast in a fight, such as one that takes a minute or more to cast.
- **Spell baseline.** The SRD lift numbers are a committed table under `data/baseline/`, regenerated on purpose. It records fingerprints of the effects file, party, panel, policy, engine and fight count. A homebrew run fights only its own spell and the control. A report computed against a stale table says so, as the monster curve does.
- **Spell baseline, as built (spell-testing ticket 016).** `bench baseline generate --spells` runs every modeled SRD spell through the lift runner against the committed panel and writes `data/baseline/spell-lift.json`, embedded in the binary as the curve is. Each spell keeps its rows with the member-by-member detail dropped, so a row carries its lift and interval, single and group columns, control win rate, casts and per-cast diagnostics, or its `never_cast` or `not_measured` reason. The table records the effects file hash, the panel fingerprint, the fight count and the curve's four fingerprints (fixture set, party, policy, engine), and `SpellBaseline::stale_reasons` names each one that differs from a run's. Generation refuses a panel that is stale against the current inputs, because every row would be stale at birth. It uses no assumptions, `unspecified` lighting and the panel's fights per member, as the curve does. `bench baseline check --spells` (`check` is `status`) adds the table's comparison to the curve's. Plain `check` reports the curve and the panel as before.
