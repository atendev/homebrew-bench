# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- `bench action <actor> <action-id> <target-id>... --slot <level>` casts every leveled spell in the action at that level and spends a slot of that level. The engine refuses it, with the reason, when the stat block has no `spell_slots`, the action casts no leveled spell, the level is below a spell's level, or no slot of that level is left. Command histories written before the flag still replay.
- An upcast of a spell whose higher-slot text the engine can't apply logs an `upcast_clause` event, `<actor> casts <spell> at level <n>; agent clause: <text>`, and a batch warns once per such cast in the monster's stat block (`Spell <id> at level <n>: <clause> is not applied; results may understate this monster.`).
- `bench move --opportunity <reactor>[:<attack-id>]` has a reactor take its Opportunity Attack before the move, rolled by the engine. Repeat the flag for several reactors. The default attack is the reactor's first at-will melee attack. The reaction is spent hit or miss and returns on the reactor's own turn. A mover left unable to act stops without moving. A named reactor that can't react fails the move with the reason. Batch results are unchanged. See ADR 0015.

- `bench disengage <actor>` takes the Disengage action: it spends the action, and the actor's moves for the rest of the turn provoke nothing. A flag on the actor lasts until its turn ends.
- Flyby and Agile (nine SRD monsters) import as the `no_provoke` effect, so those creatures never provoke an Opportunity Attack. Flyby is not limited to flying out of reach. Batch numbers are unchanged and batch still lists these traits as skipped mechanics; the baseline curve was regenerated for the new fixture fingerprint with identical numbers. See ADR 0015.

- `bench end-turn --legendary <holder>:<action-id>[=<target>,...]` takes legendary actions after the turn ends and before the next one starts. Repeat the flag for several; they resolve in order and each costs one use. The creature whose turn is ending can't take one, and the creature whose turn comes next can. Recharge, cooldown and per-day limits on a legendary action now apply. A declaration that is not allowed fails the whole end-turn with the reason. A holder that drops partway loses its remaining uses. Each use logs `LEGENDARY ACTION <holder>: <name> (<used> of <budget>)`. See ADR 0016.
- The DM digest has a `legendary` list: who may take a legendary action when the active turn ends, with uses left and ready actions. The public digest leaves it out.

### Changed

- Batch takes legendary actions between the end of one turn and the start of the next. Before, a monster whose turn came next spent from a budget that had already reset, and its recharge and death-save rolls came first. Batch numbers for monsters with legendary actions change. The engine version is now 0.1.1, so `bench baseline status` and batch reports call the baseline curve stale until the next regeneration.
- 44 SRD legendary actions that say the creature can't take them again until the start of its next turn now import with the `cooldown_until_next_turn` usage, mostly on adult and ancient dragons, the lich, the vampire and the mummy lord. Live mode refuses a second use in one round. Batch spends each at most once a round, so batch numbers can change for the 15 monsters whose cooldown action is modeled: 14 adult and ancient dragons and the lich. `bench baseline status` also reports the embedded fixture set as changed until the next regeneration.
- The `opportunity_attack` notice now names only creatures that could still react: enemies that can act, can take reactions, have their reaction, and can see the mover. It no longer reports "has no reaction available", and allies never get it. A reactor that can't see the mover gets an `assumption` line instead.
- Attack rolls now follow the PHB rule for Advantage and Disadvantage: a roll has at most one of each, and with both it is a straight roll, however many conditions, Invisible checks or timed modifiers give each. Before, the result depended on the order the sources were read, so an Invisible, Poisoned attacker against a Paralyzed target rolled with Advantage. Batch numbers can change for fights where an attack had sources on both sides. The engine version is now 0.1.2, so `bench baseline status` and batch reports call the baseline curve stale until the next regeneration.
- Spells resolve at their cast level: the slot the cast names, or the spell's own level. Each spell effects entry now has a `level`, and its scaling rules (`extra_dice`, `extra_modifier`, `extra_targets` and `extra_effects`) apply once per level above it. The 14 adult and ancient dragon fixtures that cast a "level N version" spell (Acid Arrow, Guiding Bolt, Ice Knife, Mind Spike, Scorching Ray or Shatter) now cast it at that level, so their batch numbers change. Higher-slot text the engine can't apply moved from `notes` and `agent_clauses` to a new `upcast_clause`, which changes 38 standard party files, and the party's Dominate Person and Dominate Monster casts above their level are now marked `upcast`. The engine version is now 0.1.3, and `bench baseline status` also reports the party as changed until the next regeneration.
- Magic Missile, Heal, Disintegrate, Finger of Death and Regenerate now resolve. Their dice strings could not be rolled before: batch's Heal casts failed, Magic Missile, Disintegrate and Finger of Death scored nothing so batch never chose them, and no standard party member casts Regenerate. Heal is a flat 70 plus 10 per level above 6 and adds no spellcasting modifier; a Heal effect's `dice` is now optional and `spellcasting_modifier: false` turns the modifier off. Prayer of Healing and Regenerate no longer add the modifier either, matching the SRD. Batch numbers for caster and healer cells can change.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-02

### Added

- `bench batch <monster.json|fixture id>` runs one monster against the standard party, or `--party <name>`, at each level in `--levels` (default 1-20). It plays `--fights` fights per level (default 100) with a `--max-rounds` cap (default 20). Stdout is a JSON summary. The command writes `report.html` and three sampled fight logs to `--out`, or by default to `reports/<monster>-seed-<seed>/` under the output directory.
- `bench batch --seed <s> --fight <i>` replays one fight and prints its full log. Fight indexes run across levels, so index `i` is level `i / fights + 1`.
- Batch refuses a stat block with unmodeled abilities or agent clauses that have no SRD default resolution, and lists every offending id under `unmodeled_ability_refused`. `--assume abilities.<id>=ignored` clears one. `--assume policy.<constant>=<value>` changes a tactics policy constant, and `--assumptions <file>` reads a JSON list of `{path, value, reason}`.
- Both sides of a batch fight use tactics policy `homebrew-bench.tactics` v1. Each decision writes a `DECISION` log line with its reason, targets, resource, movement and area assumption.
- `data/baseline/curve.json` is the committed CR baseline curve: all 341 SRD fixtures fought 100 times per party level 1 to 20 by the standard party under tactics policy v2, with per-`(CR, level)` cells, input fingerprints, and thin-data labels. 174 fixtures carry agent clauses or unmodeled abilities and fight with those left out, listed per fixture under `ignored_abilities`. `bench baseline generate` rebuilds it (rebuild `bench` after changing `data/` first) and `bench baseline status` compares its fingerprints with the ones this binary computes.
- `bench batch` now compares the run against the curve: a claimed-CR verdict in both directions (including "no threat at this level"), whole-level effective CR, rounds and damage-per-round anomalies against the fixture spread with thin cells named, a basis line, the SRD XP and encounter-budget facts, and a stale-baseline warning when the fixtures, party, tactics policy or engine differ from the curve's.
- Fixtures, the spell effects file, the standard party and the baseline are embedded in `bench` at compile time. `bench fixture list`, `bench fixture show <id>` and `bench party show [--level <1-20>]` print them, and a bare fixture or standard party id works wherever `bench start`, `bench add` and `bench batch` take a stat block path.
- Batch warns when a monster casts a spell the effects file lacks, since those casts do nothing.
- The `homebrew-bench` agent skill in `.agents/skills/homebrew-bench/` teaches any agent batch mode, live mode and house rules, with a house-rule template. `.claude/skills/homebrew-bench` links to it and `AGENTS.md` points to it.
- `install.sh` (macOS, Linux) and `install.ps1` (Windows) install `bench`, `NOTICE`, `LICENSE` and the skill into `~/.bench/`, then copy the skill to `~/.agents/skills/` and to `~/.claude/`, `~/.cline/` and `~/.kiro/` when those agents are present. They take `--no-skills`, `--agent <name>`, `--version <tag>` and `--from-source`, verify the release checksum, build the checkout when run from a clone, clone and build the requested release when no archive fits the platform, exit 2 on a usage error, and keep `config.toml`, `index`, `parties/`, `house-rules/`, `encounters/` and `reports/` on re-run. `update.sh` re-runs the installer.
- Release archives for macOS (x86_64, arm64), Linux (x86_64, arm64) and Windows (x86_64) are built by cargo-dist and hold `bench`, `NOTICE`, `LICENSE` and the `homebrew-bench` skill.
- Root `README.md`, `LICENSE` (MIT) and `NOTICE`, which carries the SRD 5.2.1 CC-BY-4.0 attribution, a note of the changes made to the SRD material, and the 5e-database MIT notice.

### Changed

- Effective CR reads "around 1 or trivial" when a monster is already Low at level 1 (was "1 or lower").
- The tactics policy no longer recurses without end when two combatants each carry condition effects; a control effect nested more than two threat evaluations deep counts as zero. This fixed a stack overflow on the Dryad and moves the policy to v2.
- The `replay` command on each batch sample, and on `bench batch --fight` output, now reruns the fight for a homebrew file. It keeps the monster argument, `--party`, `--assumptions`, `--assume` and `--assume-reason` as passed, adds `--levels` when the party lacks some of levels 1 to 20, and shell-quotes each value. Before, it named the monster by id and dropped the assumptions.
- A move now sets the range band on both combatants, not only the mover.
- `bench move <actor> <near|far>` with no `--target` now moves the actor to that band relative to every other combatant and breaks every engagement it leaves. It costs the largest single-pair band change and returns an `opportunity_attack` event for each engagement it breaks, leaving out combatants that are dead, unconscious or stable. Before, it spent movement and changed no positions. An untargeted move to `engaged` is now refused.
- The log now says when a creature falls unconscious or dies from damage.
- A condition can end when its creature takes damage. Sleep and Hypnotic Pattern now work this way.
- When a caster loses concentration, the conditions and modifiers it held by concentration end.

- With `--pc-dice ask`, `bench start` now prompts for each PC's initiative d20 before round 1, and the initiative line is marked `(player dice)`. A surprised PC enters two faces and keeps the lower. Under `--group-initiative`, PCs that share a stat block share one prompt. The encounter stays in round 0 with no active turn until `bench respond` answers.
- With `--pc-dice ask`, `bench add` of a PC stat block prompts for its initiative d20 and adds the PC once `bench respond` answers. Declining lets the engine roll it.
- A concentration save now draws its d20 from the action's own dice stream at the point its damage lands, so any dice the action rolls after that come after the save. Before this, the save reused a die the action had already drawn, and the stream lost the save's draw.
- In ask mode, `(player dice)` marks a line only when every die behind that event came from a face the user entered. PCs sharing a group initiative roll are all marked. Before this, one entered face marked every PC line the command produced, including dice the engine rolled after a decline. Events in JSON output carry `"player_dice": true` when marked. Heal, revive, temporary hit point and death save events follow the same rule.
- Granting temporary hit points now logs a `temp_hp` event, `<target> gains <N> temporary HP`, in live logs and batch fight logs. The public view drops the amount for monsters. Batch results and dice are unchanged.
- A death save event now carries its d20 in `roll`, so the line ends in `[d20 N]`.
- `bench override --standing` records the override as a standing assumption. Its OVERRIDE line ends in `[standing assumption]`. Every later event line whose actor or target is the assumption's subject ends in `[standing assumption: <path>]`. The subject is the combatant the path starts with, or, for `stat_blocks.<id>` paths, every combatant using that stat block. Assumptions on other state paths, such as `round` or `settings`, are recorded but tag no event lines. Any later override on the same path replaces the assumption, or drops it when the later override is not `--standing`.

### Replay compatibility

- Encounters recorded before the batch runner can diverge under `bench replay` at their first move, first drop to 0 HP, first damage to a sleeping creature, or first lost concentration, because each of these now changes the state differently.
- Encounters recorded before this change can diverge under `bench replay` from the first concentration save onward, because later dice come from different stream positions. Ask-mode encounters that used `bench add` for a PC diverge at that add, which now raises a prompt instead of rolling. Their starting initiative does not diverge, because their initial snapshot already holds it. Ask-mode encounters recorded after this change start from a snapshot taken before initiative, and replay rolls initiative again from the recorded `bench respond`.
- Encounters that used `bench move` without `--target` diverge under `bench replay` at that move, which now changes positions, or fail there when the move was to `engaged`.
