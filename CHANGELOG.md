# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- A `teleport` effect takes `"subject": "self" | "target"`, `self` by default. `self` is the creature using the ability and `target` is each target the effect resolves against, allowed at the top level of an ability used with targets and under an attack's `on_hit` or a save's `on_failure`. A top-level `target` teleport used with no target is refused. Stat blocks, states and command histories without the field load as `self` and serialize without it. See ADR 0005.
- `bench batch` warns once for each ability and engine error behind a plan the engine refused, counted over every fight rather than only the sampled logs, most frequent first: `Ogre: Greatclub failed 62 times (invalid dice expression 70); the action was lost each time`. The error is blamed on the ability that raised it. When a creature's reaction (an Opportunity Attack, Shield or Parry) fails while another creature's plan resolves, the row names the reactor: `Ogre: Greatclub failed 50 times as a reaction during another creature's action (invalid dice expression 70); the action was lost each time`. The tail names what was lost (the action, bonus action or legendary action), or says `a recomputed plan ran instead each time`, or splits the two (`the action was lost 2 times and a recomputed plan ran the other time`). A refused action or bonus action is retried once, so an ability that fails every time counts two failures per lost action. Batch numbers, the dice stream and the logs don't change.
- Haste's extra action is modeled. A Hasted creature takes one extra action on each of its turns with `bench action <actor> <action-id> <target-id>... --extra` (one attack: an action that is exactly one single-target attack) or `bench disengage <actor> --extra`, and the action stays unused. A second extra action, a Multiattack, a spell or a two-target attack is refused with the reason, as is `--extra` without an extra action. Two Hastes on one creature still give one extra action. The DM digest shows `extra_action` with the active combatant's uses while one is left. Dash, Hide and Utilize remain an agent clause.
- Haste's lethargy is modeled. When a Haste ends, its target is Incapacitated and has Speed 0 until the end of its next turn; if Haste ends during the target's own turn, that turn doesn't count.
- Homebrew vocabulary: the modifier target `extra_action` with an `only` list (`one_attack`, `disengage`, or empty for any action), `on_end` effects on a `timed_modifier` that land on its bearer when it ends, and the durations `until_end_of_next_turn` and `until_end_of_turn`.
- Live mode opens reaction windows (ADR 0018). A move that leaves a creature's reach holds on a `reaction` prompt for each creature that could make an Opportunity Attack, in initiative order, before any attack resolves. A hit by an attack roll holds the command on a prompt for the creature hit when it has a ready reaction with a hit trigger. `bench respond --take [<id>]` takes the reaction and `bench respond --decline` passes, and the held command runs again with the answer. A taken hit reaction resolves before damage, and the engine checks the hit again against the new AC; a natural 20 still hits. The log adds `PROMPT <message> answered: takes <name>` or `declined`, then `REACTION <reactor>: <name> (hit by <attacker>)`. The public view hides a monster's window unless it takes the reaction.
- A reaction entry may carry `trigger: {hit_by_attack_roll: {attack: any | melee | ranged}}`, and the duration `triggering_attack` ends once the attack that opened the window resolves. A `trigger` outside the reactions list, or a `triggering_attack` duration on an entry without a hit trigger, fails at load.
- Parry on 7 SRD fixtures (bandit captain, erinyes, gladiator, knight, marilith, noble, warrior veteran) and the pirate captain's Riposte now import as a melee hit trigger with an AC bonus for that attack. Holding a weapon is assumed, and Riposte's extra attack stays an agent clause.
- The standard party's Shield carries a hit trigger, so a caster hit by an attack roll gets a Shield window. This changes 20 standard caster files.
- `bench start --lighting <unspecified|dim_light|bright_light|sunlight>` and `bench batch --lighting <...>` set one lighting value for the whole encounter, `unspecified` by default. `bench override settings.lighting <value> --reason <text>` changes it mid-fight, and undo and replay cover it. The DM digest and the public digest carry `lighting`, and the START log line names it. A batch report gains a Lighting row in Standing assumptions, the sample logs print `lighting = <value>`, the JSON summary carries `lighting`, and each sample's replay command adds `--lighting` when it isn't the default. Outside `unspecified`, the verdict's basis line says the baseline curve fought with lighting unspecified.
- `roll_modifier` effects on a trait now apply while the encounter's lighting meets them: sunlight counts as Bright Light, and `unspecified` turns no lighting trait on. Attack rolls and every saving throw (including concentration and repeat saves) take them, and the event names the trait, as `(Disadvantage: Sunlight Sensitivity, sunlight)`. A save with Advantage or Disadvantage from lighting rolls a second d20 (in ask mode the player enters both); a straight save draws one d20 as before. Each lighting trait gets a batch finding, "Applies in sunlight; on in this run." or "...; off in this run."
- 11 SRD fixtures now model their lighting trait as roll modifiers: Sunlight Sensitivity on the kobold warrior, drider, specter, wight and wraith, Sunlight Weakness on the shadow, Light Sensitivity on the cloaker, and the Sunlight trait on the vampire spawn and the three vampire forms. The vampires' 20 Radiant damage at the start of a turn in sunlight stays an agent clause with its `skipped` default.
- The `Lighting` value `dim_light` is new.
- `bench action <actor> <action-id> <target-id>... --slot <level>` casts every leveled spell in the action at that level and spends a slot of that level. The engine refuses it, with the reason, when the stat block has no `spell_slots`, the action casts no leveled spell, the level is below a spell's level, or no slot of that level is left. Command histories written before the flag still replay.
- An upcast of a spell whose higher-slot text the engine can't apply logs an `upcast_clause` event, `<actor> casts <spell> at level <n>; agent clause: <text>`, and a batch warns once per such cast in the monster's stat block (`Spell <id> at level <n>: <clause> is not applied; results may understate this monster.`).
- `bench move --opportunity <reactor>[:<attack-id>]` has a reactor take its Opportunity Attack before the move, rolled by the engine. Repeat the flag for several reactors. The default attack is the reactor's first at-will melee attack. The reaction is spent hit or miss and returns on the reactor's own turn. A mover left unable to act stops without moving. A named reactor that can't react fails the move with the reason. Batch results are unchanged. See ADR 0015.
- `bench disengage <actor>` takes the Disengage action: it spends the action, and the actor's moves for the rest of the turn provoke nothing. A flag on the actor lasts until its turn ends.
- Flyby and Agile (nine SRD monsters) import as the `no_provoke` effect, so those creatures never provoke an Opportunity Attack. Flyby is not limited to flying out of reach. Batch numbers are unchanged and batch still lists these traits as skipped mechanics; the baseline curve was regenerated for the new fixture fingerprint with identical numbers. See ADR 0015.
- `bench end-turn --legendary <holder>:<action-id>[=<target>,...]` takes legendary actions after the turn ends and before the next one starts. Repeat the flag for several; they resolve in order and each costs one use. The creature whose turn is ending can't take one, and the creature whose turn comes next can. Recharge, cooldown and per-day limits on a legendary action now apply. A declaration that is not allowed fails the whole end-turn with the reason. A holder that drops partway loses its remaining uses. Each use logs `LEGENDARY ACTION <holder>: <name> (<used> of <budget>)`. See ADR 0016.
- The DM digest has a `legendary` list: who may take a legendary action when the active turn ends, with uses left and ready actions. The public digest leaves it out.

### Changed

- The engine is 0.1.22. An override that leaves a combatant id the encounter didn't have, such as a `combatants` list with a new entry or with an entry's id changed, is now refused whole with an error naming `bench add`. Before, it succeeded and the new creature got no initiative, never took a turn and logged no ADD line. Live only: batch numbers don't move, and the curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.21. An override that drops a creature from `combatants` (clearing `combatants.<id>` or `combatants.<index>`, or setting `combatants` without it) now cleans up as `bench remove` does: the creature leaves `initiative_order` and `initiative_rolls`, and other creatures' engagements and ranges. Before, the override left the id in all of those. Setting `combatants` to a list that leaves out a concentrating creature now also ends the conditions its spell held on the creatures that stay. Before, their entries in the new list put those conditions back. Two override removals are refused: one that drops the creature holding the active turn, with a pointer to `bench remove`, which ends the turn first, and any removal while a prompt is pending, as `bench remove` already was. Live only: batch numbers don't move, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.20. Removing a concentrating creature now ends its concentration first, so the conditions and timed modifiers its spell held on others end with it and the log says `<id> loses concentration` after the REMOVE or OVERRIDE line. That covers `bench remove <id>` and an override that clears `combatants.<id>` or `combatants.<index>` (or replaces `combatants` without it). A creature whose `concentration` was already cleared by override still ends the concentration-duration conditions and modifiers it is the source of, logged as `<id> leaves; the effects it held by concentration end`. Before, what it held stayed on its bearers with a source no longer in the encounter. Live only: batch numbers don't move, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.19. `bench override` now ends the concentration of any creature it leaves unable to concentrate: one with an incapacitating condition (Incapacitated, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned, Unconscious), one whose `end_state` becomes Unconscious, Stable or Dead, and one given a `concentration` while already down or incapacitated. It logs `<id> loses concentration`, as an effect that applies the condition already did. Before, the agent had to end it by hand. Only a creature the override changed is checked: an able creature keeps its concentration, and a creature already down with a stale concentration is left alone by an override elsewhere. Live only: batch numbers don't move, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.18. `bench override` now ends the concentration of a creature that an override leaves with an incapacitating condition (Incapacitated, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned, Unconscious), and logs `<id> loses concentration`, as an effect that applies the condition already did. Before, the agent had to end it by hand. An override that leaves the conditions alone doesn't touch concentration. Live only: batch numbers don't move, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.17. A stat block whose cast of a cantrip names any `slot_level`, 0 included, now fails at load with `<ability-id>: a cantrip's cast names a slot level`. The engine already refused such a cast when it resolved, so the batch policy skipped it without a word. No SRD fixture or standard party file has such a cast, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.16. The batch policy counts a cast with no `slot_level` as using a slot of the spell's level when the caster has `spell_slots`, as the engine already spends one. Before, such a cast scored as unlimited: no resource cost, no limited-use bar against at-will plans, and no slot in the plan's reason. A cast that names a slot level counts as before. No SRD fixture or standard party file has such a cast, and the regenerated curve differs only in its engine version.
- The engine is 0.1.15. A teleport now moves the creature using it by default and clears all of that creature's engagements, in batch and live, and logs `<id> teleports up to 30 feet`. Before, it cleared the engagements of the command's targets, so a batch teleport moved an opponent and a live teleport with no target did nothing. Under policy v3 a teleport plan targets nobody, and a `target` teleport scores 0. No SRD fixture or standard party file models a teleport, so batch numbers on them are unchanged; the shift shows on homebrew stat blocks. `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.14. A ranged attack roll has Disadvantage when the attacker is within 5 feet of an enemy that can see it and isn't Incapacitated (PHB ch. 1, "Ranged Attacks in Close Combat"), in live and batch mode. "Within 5 feet" means engaged. An Invisible attacker is unseen by an enemy without Blindsight or Truesight, a Blinded enemy without Blindsight sees nothing, and a downed or dead enemy doesn't count. An attack with both reach and range, such as a thrown dagger or javelin, is a melee attack against a target the attacker is engaged with and a ranged attack against any other. The attack event names the source, as `(Disadvantage: ranged attack in close combat)`. Under policy v3 the batch policy reads this rule from the engine and drops its own version, which ignored sight and Incapacitated. Batch numbers change. `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.13. Under policy v3, the batch policy now values a buff by the rounds the fight has left and the chance the caster keeps concentration, and counts Haste's lethargy as a lost turn. The rounds left are the opponents' hit points over the damage the caster's side deals in a round, at most two. Each round counts only as far as the caster is expected to still hold concentration by then; the first round is safe only when the bearer is another creature that acts before any opponent. Holding it means passing its Constitution saves against the hits and save damage it expects from opponents that aim at it and the areas of the rest, and is ruled out when that damage would drop it. A concentration buff whose end incapacitates its bearer costs the bearer's threat times the chance concentration breaks within those rounds. The decision line adds both when they apply: `haste on standard-striker-05 (+2 AC against 20.8 attack damage, advantage on dex saves against 0.0 dex save damage, extra action 15.0, 1.3 of 1.8 rounds at keep 0.34, lethargy -12.9)`. The standard caster casts Haste far less, almost never against monsters the party beats in one or two rounds, and batch numbers change. The batch report's method sentence says so. `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.12. Under policy v3, batch now scores a teleport by the Opportunity Attacks it avoids: the attacks the policy would take from the creatures engaged with the actor, logged as `teleports clear of 5.5 opportunity damage`. The policy reads Advantage and Disadvantage on attacks from the same sources the engine rolls with, including Prone by engagement after the plan's move, Grappled and Frightened by source, Invisible through the sight rule and downed PCs, and any Advantage and Disadvantage together now make a straight roll. Its save chances count automatic Strength and Dexterity failures, Restrained's Dexterity Disadvantage, lighting and timed save modifiers such as Haste. No SRD fixture models a teleport, so the shift shows on homebrew stat blocks and in cells where these conditions occur. `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.11. Under policy v3, batch now scores buffs, Haste's extra action and pushes. A spell or effect that raises AC, saving throws or attack rolls is valued by the damage it prevents or adds over two rounds, on whichever of the caster and its living allies it helps most. AC counts only attack-roll damage and save Advantage only damage from saves of that ability. The decision line names the bearer: `haste on standard-striker-11 (+2 AC against 51.5 attack damage, advantage on dex saves against 0.0 dex save damage, extra action 21.1)`. A bearer that can't act, such as one in Haste's lethargy, gets no value for the extra action. A Hasted creature takes one extra attack on each of its turns after its action and bonus action, logged as `DECISION <id> extra action: ...`, and a failed one is reported as a lost extra action. A push scores a quarter of the target's threat, or all of it when it sends a target slower than 35 feet to far, if every attack the target has needs engagement. A caster keeps its concentration while it holds a modifier on anyone, so Haste is never recast onto a second ally. The standard caster now casts Haste from level 5, so standard-party cells move: against the adult red dragon the party's win rate drops from 100% to 92% at level 17 (35% against 34% at level 11), and fights against the ogre and the knight run longer. The batch report's method sentence says what is scored. `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.

- A stat block whose cast (an action's `cast`, a Multiattack step's, or one nested in another effect) names a `slot_level` below the spell's level now fails at load with `<ability-id>: a cast's slot level is below the spell's level`. Such a cast could never be used, so the batch policy skipped it without a word. `bench start`, `bench add`, `bench batch` and `bench schema validate` stop before any dice are drawn. Every SRD fixture and standard party file already passes, and the engine stays 0.1.10.
- The engine is 0.1.10. A downed PC, dying or Stable, now dies outright when one hit's damage, after resistance, immunity and Temporary Hit Points, equals or exceeds its Hit Point maximum, and the log reads `<id> dies from massive damage`. A lesser hit gives a death save failure as before, two on a critical. Temporary Hit Points on a downed PC now absorb damage first. A creature that drops to 0 Hit Points, falls unconscious or dies loses concentration with no Constitution save (`<id> loses concentration`), and so does a creature given the Incapacitated, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned or Unconscious condition. Live encounters saved by 0.1.9 load, and replaying them diverges where such a hit lands. Batch numbers may change; `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.9. Saving throws now follow the 2024 conditions. Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned and Unconscious creatures and downed PCs fail Strength and Dexterity saves automatically, repeat saves included: no d20 is drawn, ask mode prompts no player, the save event reads `<id> fails the save automatically (paralyzed, Dexterity)`, and the failed save's effects and damage apply as usual. A Restrained creature has Disadvantage on Dexterity saves. Timed Advantage and Disadvantage save modifiers now apply to their bearer's saves, so Haste gives its target Advantage on Dexterity saves. A Haste cast on 0.1.5 or earlier, whose modifiers sit on the caster, still gives no save Advantage. A roll's note now names every source behind its Advantage or Disadvantage, as `(Disadvantage: restrained)` or `(Advantage: haste)`, and a straight roll names none, so an attack or save whose lighting trait is cancelled no longer names the trait. Live encounters saved by 0.1.8 load, and replaying them diverges where one of these rules applies. Batch numbers change wherever these conditions meet a Strength or Dexterity save; `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.8. Attack rolls now follow the 2024 conditions (ADR 0017). Blinded and Petrified targets grant Advantage, and Restrained attackers roll with Disadvantage. A Grappled attacker has Disadvantage against everyone but the grappler named in the condition's `source`. A Prone target grants Advantage only to an engaged attacker and gives Disadvantage to anyone else, ranged attackers included. A Frightened attacker has Disadvantage only while it can see the condition's `source`. Invisible counts only against an observer without Blindsight or Truesight. A downed PC counts as Unconscious and Prone. A hit on a Paralyzed or Unconscious target (or a downed PC) from an engaged attacker is a Critical Hit, and the attack event says why, as `(critical: paralyzed, within 5 feet)`. Damage to a Stable PC now gives it a death save failure (two on a critical) and logs `<id> is no longer stable` as it starts dying again. Opportunity Attacks use the same sight rule, so a reactor with Blindsight or Truesight can strike an Invisible mover and a Blinded reactor with Blindsight can react. Live encounters saved by 0.1.7 load, and replaying them diverges where one of these rules applies. Batch numbers change wherever these conditions come up; `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The engine is 0.1.7. Live encounters saved by 0.1.6 load and play as before: a Haste cast on 0.1.6 grants no extra action and brings no lethargy. Replaying or undoing past such a cast re-runs it with the new Haste and diverges there. Batch numbers are unchanged (policy v3 never casts Haste); `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- Timed modifiers land on their target. Before, every modifier landed on the creature that used the ability, so Slow gave its caster -2 AC and no reactions, and Hypnotic Pattern gave its caster Speed 0. A `timed_modifier` now takes `"bearer": "target"` (the default) or `"self"`, and the standard party's Shield, Haste on an ally, Slow and Hypnotic Pattern each land where the spell says. A top-level `target` modifier used with no target is refused with "<ability> needs a target for its modifier". The modifier event reads `<bearer> gains <ability> from <source>`. Parry on the 7 SRD fixtures and Riposte now carry `"bearer": "self"`.
- Migration for homebrew: a top-level `timed_modifier` with no `bearer` used to change the creature itself and now changes the ability's target. Add `"bearer": "self"` to every self-buff, such as a reaction that adds to the creature's AC. A `triggering_attack` modifier (Parry) needs no change. `bench schema validate` now returns a `warnings` list naming each top-level modifier with no `bearer`.
- One spell's modifiers on one creature apply once: with two Hastes on an ally, only one +2 AC and one doubled Speed apply, and the other takes over when the first caster's concentration ends. The most potent applies, else the most recent. Potency is the size of the change: a -3 beats a -1, and a `set` on Speed farthest from the creature's walk speed wins (0 over 10 on a 30-foot walker, 60 over 40).
- Slow's agent clause now tells the agent to end Slow on a target that succeeds on its repeat save, by clearing that target's Slow modifiers with `bench override`.
- Speed modifiers now work in the engine, the batch policy and live mode, including `bench move` without `--feet`: Haste doubles Speed, Slow halves it, and Hypnotic Pattern sets it to 0 until the creature takes damage (`"ends_on_damage": true` on a modifier). Live mode's default move distance is also 0 for a Grappled or Restrained creature, as the policy already read it.
- Fly is now an agent clause ("Target gains a Fly Speed of 60 feet and can hover for the duration"), because the engine has no Fly Speed; before, it doubled walk speed, which did nothing.
- The engine is 0.1.6. Live encounters saved by 0.1.5 still load and resume as they played: each modifier in them stays on the creature that made it, and their Speed modifiers stay inert, so a caster who cast Slow or Hypnotic Pattern on 0.1.5 isn't halved or rooted. New modifiers always record their bearer, which is how the engine tells them apart. Batch numbers are unchanged (policy v3 never casts a buff), but `bench baseline status` reports the engine as changed until the curve is regenerated.
- The batch tactics policy is now v3 and takes reactions (ADR 0012). Batch encounters open reaction windows, and the policy takes Shield or Parry only when the reaction turns the hit into a miss, and an Opportunity Attack only when the attack is worth more than the reactor's reaction reserve. Each decided window logs a reason line, held reactions included. Batch numbers move: the standard caster now shields hits, and fixtures with Parry survive longer.
- The step-out penalty a ranged plan pays for leaving melee now counts only the Opportunity Attacks the policy would really take: melee attacks from reactors that pass the engine's eligibility rule (no Disengage, no Flyby or Agile, a reactor that can see and react) and that would spend their reaction on it. Before, it counted ranged attacks too, and the engine never delivered that damage.
- `bench baseline status` and batch reports call the baseline curve stale (the policy is v3 and the curve used v2) until the next regeneration.
- Batch numbers are unchanged: batch encounters open no reaction windows, and the policy still takes no reactions. The engine version is now 0.1.5, because a live encounter started on it can replay into reaction prompts an older engine never opened. Live encounters saved by 0.1.4 still load and replay as recorded, with no windows. `bench baseline status` reports the fixture set and the party as changed until the next regeneration.
- The `Lighting` value `normal` is now `unspecified`. Old homebrew and state files holding `normal` still load, and state files with no lighting load as `unspecified`.
- A `roll_modifier` outside a trait's top-level effects (in an action, bonus action, reaction or legendary action, or inside an attack, save or area) or keyed to `unspecified` now fails the stat block at load, before any dice are drawn. Before, a `roll_modifier` in an action pushed a concentration modifier and read no lighting.
- Batch numbers under the default lighting are unchanged: every lighting trait is off when the lighting is unspecified. The engine version is now 0.1.4.
- Batch takes legendary actions between the end of one turn and the start of the next. Before, a monster whose turn came next spent from a budget that had already reset, and its recharge and death-save rolls came first. Batch numbers for monsters with legendary actions change. The engine version is now 0.1.1, so `bench baseline status` and batch reports call the baseline curve stale until the next regeneration.
- 44 SRD legendary actions that say the creature can't take them again until the start of its next turn now import with the `cooldown_until_next_turn` usage, mostly on adult and ancient dragons, the lich, the vampire and the mummy lord. Live mode refuses a second use in one round. Batch spends each at most once a round, so batch numbers can change for the 15 monsters whose cooldown action is modeled: 14 adult and ancient dragons and the lich. `bench baseline status` also reports the embedded fixture set as changed until the next regeneration.
- The `opportunity_attack` notice now names only creatures that could still react: enemies that can act, can take reactions, have their reaction, and can see the mover. It no longer reports "has no reaction available", and allies never get it. A reactor that can't see the mover gets an `assumption` line instead.
- Attack rolls now follow the PHB rule for Advantage and Disadvantage: a roll has at most one of each, and with both it is a straight roll, however many conditions, Invisible checks or timed modifiers give each. Before, the result depended on the order the sources were read, so an Invisible, Poisoned attacker against a Paralyzed target rolled with Advantage. Batch numbers can change for fights where an attack had sources on both sides. The engine version is now 0.1.2, so `bench baseline status` and batch reports call the baseline curve stale until the next regeneration.
- Spells resolve at their cast level: the slot the cast names, or the spell's own level. Each spell effects entry now has a `level`, and its scaling rules (`extra_dice`, `extra_modifier`, `extra_targets` and `extra_effects`) apply once per level above it. The 14 adult and ancient dragon fixtures that cast a "level N version" spell (Acid Arrow, Guiding Bolt, Ice Knife, Mind Spike, Scorching Ray or Shatter) now cast it at that level, so their batch numbers change. Higher-slot text the engine can't apply moved from `notes` and `agent_clauses` to a new `upcast_clause`, which changes 38 standard party files, and the party's Dominate Person and Dominate Monster casts above their level are now marked `upcast`. The engine version is now 0.1.3, and `bench baseline status` also reports the party as changed until the next regeneration.
- Magic Missile, Heal, Disintegrate, Finger of Death and Regenerate now resolve. Their dice strings could not be rolled before: batch's Heal casts failed, Magic Missile, Disintegrate and Finger of Death scored nothing so batch never chose them, and no standard party member casts Regenerate. Heal is a flat 70 plus 10 per level above 6 and adds no spellcasting modifier; a Heal effect's `dice` is now optional and `spellcasting_modifier: false` turns the modifier off. Prayer of Healing and Regenerate no longer add the modifier either, matching the SRD. Batch numbers for caster and healer cells can change.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-02

### Added

- `bench batch <monster.json|fixture id>` runs one monster against the standard party, or `--party <name>`, at each level in `--levels` (default 1-20). It plays `--fights` fights per level (default 100) with a `--max-rounds` cap (default 20). Stdout is a JSON summary. The command writes `report.html` and three sampled fight logs to `--out`, or by default to `reports/<monster>-seed-<seed>/` under the output directory.
- `bench batch --seed <s> --fight <i>` replays one fight and prints its full log. Fight indexes run across levels, so index `i` is level `i / fights + 1`.
- Batch refuses a stat block with unmodeled abilities or agent clauses that have no SRD default resolution, and lists every offending id under `unmodeled_ability_refused`. `--assume abilities.<id>=ignored` clears one. `--assume policy.<constant>=<value>` changes a tactics policy constant, and `--assumptions <file>` reads a JSON list of `{path, value, reason}`.
- Both sides of a batch fight use tactics policy `homebrew-bench.tactics` v1. Each decision writes a `DECISION` log line with its reason, targets, resource, movement and area assumption.
- `data/baseline/curve.json` is the committed CR baseline curve: all 341 SRD fixtures fought 100 times per party level 1 to 20 by the standard party under tactics policy v2, with per-`(CR, level)` cells, input fingerprints, and thin-data labels. 174 fixtures carry agent clauses or unmodeled abilities and fight with those left out, listed per fixture under `ignored_abilities`. `bench baseline generate` rebuilds it (rebuild `bench` after changing `data/` first) and `bench baseline status` compares its fingerprints with the ones this binary computes.
- `bench batch` now compares the run against the curve: a claimed-CR verdict in both directions (including "no threat at this level"), whole-level effective CR, rounds and damage-per-round anomalies against the fixture spread with thin cells named, a basis line, the SRD XP and encounter-budget facts, and a stale-baseline warning when the fixtures, party, tactics policy or engine differ from the curve's.
- Fixtures, the spell effects file, the standard party and the baseline are embedded in `bench` at compile time. `bench fixture list`, `bench fixture show <id>` and `bench party show [--level <1-20>]` print them, and a bare fixture or standard party id works wherever `bench start`, `bench add` and `bench batch` take a stat block path.
- Batch warns when a monster casts a spell the effects file lacks, since those casts do nothing.
- The `homebrew-bench` agent skill in `.agents/skills/homebrew-bench/` teaches any agent batch mode, live mode and house rules, with a house-rule template. `.claude/skills/homebrew-bench` links to it and `AGENTS.md` points to it.
- `install.sh` (macOS, Linux) and `install.ps1` (Windows) install `bench`, `NOTICE`, `LICENSE` and the skill into `~/.bench/`, then copy the skill to `~/.agents/skills/` and to `~/.claude/`, `~/.cline/` and `~/.kiro/` when those agents are present. They take `--no-skills`, `--agent <name>`, `--version <tag>` and `--from-source`, verify the release checksum, build the checkout when run from a clone, clone and build the requested release when no archive fits the platform, exit 2 on a usage error, and keep `config.toml`, `index`, `parties/`, `house-rules/`, `encounters/` and `reports/` on re-run. `update.sh` re-runs the installer.
- Release archives for macOS (x86_64, arm64), Linux (x86_64, arm64) and Windows (x86_64) are built by cargo-dist and hold `bench`, `NOTICE`, `LICENSE` and the `homebrew-bench` skill.

### Changed

- Effective CR reads "around 1 or trivial" when a monster is already Low at level 1 (was "1 or lower").
- The tactics policy no longer recurses without end when two combatants each carry condition effects; a control effect nested more than two threat evaluations deep counts as zero. This fixed a stack overflow on the Dryad and moves the policy to v2.
- The `replay` command on each batch sample, and on `bench batch --fight` output, now reruns the fight for a homebrew file. It keeps the monster argument, `--party`, `--assumptions`, `--assume` and `--assume-reason` as passed, adds `--levels` when the party lacks some of levels 1 to 20, and shell-quotes each value. Before, it named the monster by id and dropped the assumptions.
- A move now sets the range band on both combatants, not only the mover.
- `bench move <actor> <near|far>` with no `--target` now moves the actor to that band relative to every other combatant and breaks every engagement it leaves. It costs the largest single-pair band change and returns an `opportunity_attack` event for each engagement it breaks, leaving out combatants that are dead, unconscious or stable. Before, it spent movement and changed no positions. An untargeted move to `engaged` is now refused.
- The log now says when a creature falls unconscious or dies from damage.
- A condition can end when its creature takes damage. Sleep and Hypnotic Pattern now work this way.
- When a caster loses concentration, the conditions and modifiers it held by concentration end.

- With `--pc-dice ask`, `bench start` now prompts for each PC's initiative d20 before round 1, and the initiative line is marked `(player dice)`. A surprised PC enters two faces and keeps the lower. Under `--group-initiative`, PCs that share a stat block share one prompt. The encounter stays in round 0 with no active turn until `bench respond` answers.
- With `--pc-dice ask`, `bench add` of a PC stat block prompts for its initiative d20 and adds the PC once `bench respond` answers. Declining lets the engine roll it.
- A concentration save now draws its d20 from the action's own dice stream at the point its damage lands, so any dice the action rolls after that come after the save. Before this, the save reused a die the action had already drawn, and the stream lost the save's draw.
- In ask mode, `(player dice)` marks a line only when every die behind that event came from a face the user entered. PCs sharing a group initiative roll are all marked. Before this, one entered face marked every PC line the command produced, including dice the engine rolled after a decline. Events in JSON output carry `"player_dice": true` when marked. Heal, revive, temporary hit point and death save events follow the same rule.
- Granting temporary hit points now logs a `temp_hp` event, `<target> gains <N> temporary HP`, in live logs and batch fight logs. The public view drops the amount for monsters. Batch results and dice are unchanged.
- A death save event now carries its d20 in `roll`, so the line ends in `[d20 N]`.
- `bench override --standing` records the override as a standing assumption. Its OVERRIDE line ends in `[standing assumption]`. Every later event line whose actor or target is the assumption's subject ends in `[standing assumption: <path>]`. The subject is the combatant the path starts with, or, for `stat_blocks.<id>` paths, every combatant using that stat block. Assumptions on other state paths, such as `round` or `settings`, are recorded but tag no event lines. Any later override on the same path replaces the assumption, or drops it when the later override is not `--standing`.

### Replay compatibility

- Encounters recorded before the batch runner can diverge under `bench replay` at their first move, first drop to 0 HP, first damage to a sleeping creature, or first lost concentration, because each of these now changes the state differently.
- Encounters recorded before this change can diverge under `bench replay` from the first concentration save onward, because later dice come from different stream positions. Ask-mode encounters that used `bench add` for a PC diverge at that add, which now raises a prompt instead of rolling. Their starting initiative does not diverge, because their initial snapshot already holds it. Ask-mode encounters recorded after this change start from a snapshot taken before initiative, and replay rolls initiative again from the recorded `bench respond`.
- Encounters recorded before engine 0.1.18 that overrode a condition, or from before 0.1.19 an end state or concentration, onto a concentrating creature can diverge under `bench replay` at that override, which now ends the concentration, and `bench undo` replays the history under the new rule.
- Encounters recorded before engine 0.1.20 that removed a concentrating creature, by `bench remove`, by clearing it from `combatants` (by id or index), or by setting `combatants` without it, can diverge under `bench replay` at that removal, which now ends what it held, and `bench undo` replays the history under the new rule.
- Encounters recorded before engine 0.1.21 that removed a creature by override can diverge under `bench replay` at that removal, which now clears the creature's id and, for a new `combatants` list, ends the conditions a concentrating creature held. A recorded override removal of the creature holding the active turn, or one made while a prompt was pending, is now refused. `bench replay` reports it as a divergence at that entry. `bench undo` rebuilds the whole history, so it fails there with the refusal error and undoes nothing.
- Encounters recorded before engine 0.1.22 that added a combatant by override are refused at that override. `bench replay` reports it as a divergence at that entry, and `bench undo` fails there with the refusal error and undoes nothing.
- Encounters that used `bench move` without `--target` diverge under `bench replay` at that move, which now changes positions, or fail there when the move was to `engaged`.
