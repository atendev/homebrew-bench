## Question

Does the committed baseline curve show the standard party falling off SRD monsters at high levels, enough to reopen magic items in the party? Read `data/baseline/curve.json` and report, by party level, how the party's win rate and rounds against on-CR fixtures change from tier 1 to tier 4, and say whether the drop at levels 11 to 20 is beyond what the SRD's CR guidance expects.

## Notes

ADR 0010 ruled out magic items for phase 1 and said to revisit only if the curve shows this. Write the findings to `docs/research/` and link them. If the answer is yes, graduate a grilling ticket on which items to bake in; if no, rule magic items out of scope for this map.

## Resolution

Resolved 2026-11-02. Findings: [`docs/research/052-standard-party-at-high-levels.md`](../../docs/research/052-standard-party-at-high-levels.md), regenerated by `scripts/curve-tiers.py`.

The answer is split, and it does not clearly meet ADR 0010's trigger. Against on-CR fixtures the party wins 97.0%, 96.2% and 95.4% of fights in tiers 1 to 3, with deaths in about 5% of fights in each tier, and rounds stay between 4 and 7 from level 2 up. Levels 11 to 18 show no fall-off. Levels 19 and 20 read High (64.5% and 67.3% wins), which is harder than the SRD expects, since a single on-CR monster is a Low fight at every level by SRD p. 202 and the XP budget table. Those two cells rest on four and three fixtures. Two ancient dragons cause most of the shortfall, and the pit fiend reads narrowly High. Dragons as a group read a level or more above their CR from tier 2 up, while most non-dragons read at or below their CR from tier 3 up. The party gains against nearly every fixed high-CR monster from level 17 to 20.

Ruling: magic items are out of scope for this map. The high-level gap is too thin and too concentrated in a few monsters to justify changing the whole party. The dragon, pit fiend and lich gap at high CR goes into Not yet specified as fog.
